Scammers are using fake streaming websites to trick film fans into handing over their bank details — and you could be next if you're not careful.

The latest con targets people desperate to watch popular films without paying cinema prices. Fraudsters create convincing fake websites that look like legitimate streaming services, complete with genuine-looking reviews and trailers. When you try to sign up for a "free trial," they ask for your bank details — a major red flag that should make alarm bells ring.

Once they have your information, scammers can drain your account, rack up charges on your card, or use your details for identity theft. Your computer could also become infected with malware if you download anything from these dodgy sites, leaving you vulnerable to further fraud.

How to protect yourself:

Only use official streaming services you recognise — Netflix, NOW TV, Disney+, BritBox, and others you trust. If a film isn't available on legitimate platforms, it's probably not available legally online yet.

Never enter bank details on unfamiliar websites, especially for "free" services. Real streaming trials rarely ask for full payment card information upfront.

Check the website address carefully. Scammers often use URLs that look almost identical to the real thing — so look closely before you click.

Read reviews on independent websites, not just the ones on the streaming site itself. Genuine services have verified reviews on trusted platforms.

Use strong, unique passwords for any account you do create. Enable two-factor authentication for extra security.

If you've already given your details to a suspicious site, contact your bank immediately and ask them to monitor your account for fraudulent activity. Report the scam to Action Fraud on 0300 123 2040 or online at actionfraud.police.uk.

The few pounds you save by avoiding the cinema isn't worth the risk of identity theft or malware. Stick to trusted services, and your viewing habits — and your wallet — will stay safe.
