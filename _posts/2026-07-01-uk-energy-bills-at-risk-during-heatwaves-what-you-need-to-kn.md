As Britain faces more frequent and intense heatwaves, a hidden threat to your energy supply and bills is emerging: extreme heat is making crucial technology fail.

Recent events across Europe have shown just how serious this is. In June, France's Eiffel Tower closed early during a 40C heatwave, and an electric transformer in Brittany failed, leaving over 100,000 people without power. Closer to home, six NHS trusts in England declared critical incidents last week after hot weather damaged IT systems and medical equipment.

**Why does heat cause power problems?**

Power stations, transformers, solar panels, and the cables that carry electricity all struggle in extreme heat. Research from Imperial College London shows that gas-fired power stations lose roughly 10% of their output when temperatures hit 40C compared to 20C. Even solar panels – which you'd think would love sunny weather – actually become less efficient as temperatures rise above 27C.

Power cables are another weak point. Made of metal, they expand and sag in heat. When cables droop too much, they can touch trees or buildings below, causing accidents or power cuts. To prevent this during heatwaves, energy network operators deliberately reduce the amount of electricity they send along lines – which means tighter supply and potential blackouts.

**What happened in 2022?**

During July's historic heatwave, when temperatures exceeded 40C in some UK areas, overheating equipment triggered widespread power cuts. In London, data centres serving Guy's and St Thomas' hospitals went down, forcing the cancellation of operations. It was a stark reminder that this isn't just an inconvenience – it affects hospitals, homes, and businesses.

**What's being done?**

The good news: UK energy networks are currently rolling out climate resilience strategies, and experts say the UK's systems are generally coping well. The Energy Networks Association is overseeing this work to make our infrastructure more heat-resistant.

**What can you do?**

Stay informed about local resilience plans – contact your local energy network operator or check their website. Have a heatwave plan at home: charge devices beforehand, keep freezer stock, and know where your nearest community hub is. Monitor BBC Weather and local authority updates during hot spells for any power supply warnings.
