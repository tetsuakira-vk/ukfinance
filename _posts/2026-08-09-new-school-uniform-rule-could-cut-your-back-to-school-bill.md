If you're dreading the annual back-to-school uniform bill, there's good news. A new rule coming into force for the 2026 academic year could genuinely help you spend less — but only if you know about it.

From this summer, schools in England must keep branded uniform items to a bare minimum. Primary schools can require no more than three branded items, while secondary and middle schools can add a fourth if it's a tie. That might sound small, but branded blazers, jumpers and PE kits are often the priciest parts of the uniform bill.

The key is to check your school's updated policy before you buy anything. Too many parents dig out last year's uniform list and assume nothing has changed. Don't make that mistake. Download the latest policy from your school's website or ask the office directly. You may find fewer branded items are actually compulsory than before.

Once you know what must be branded, shop around for everything else. Plain white shirts, polo shirts, grey trousers, skirts, socks and tights are widely available at supermarkets — Aldi, Lidl, Asda George, Tesco F&F and Sainsbury's Tu all stock basics for much less than school suppliers. Mixing supermarket essentials with a few branded items can slash your total cost.

Don't overlook second-hand uniform either. Many schools run uniform exchanges through their PTA, or keep donated nearly-new items like blazers and PE kits available for a fraction of the original price. Ask the school office — even if they don't advertise it, a scheme may exist.

If cost is genuinely a worry, ask what financial support is available. Your local council may offer help with school clothing costs, some schools have hardship funds, and local charities often provide back-to-school support. A quick conversation could save far more than hours hunting for discounts.

The takeaway? Knowing exactly what you must buy — and what you don't — is the real money-saver. Check the new policy, buy branded only when required, shop supermarkets for basics, and ask about second-hand uniform. These four steps could leave noticeably more in your bank account when term starts.
