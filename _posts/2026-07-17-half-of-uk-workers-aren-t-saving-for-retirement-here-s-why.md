Nearly half of all working-age adults in the UK aren't putting money into a pension – and many fear they'll never be able to retire. It's a sobering reality that affects millions of people, and if you're one of them, you're not alone.

The reasons are painfully familiar: irregular work, low wages, and the struggle to afford everyday bills. Take Sarah, a 35-year-old librarian in Oxford who works two part-time jobs. She has just £5,000 saved for retirement – and she's acutely aware it's nowhere near enough. After finishing her PhD in 2020, she intended to start a pension, but a string of short-term contracts meant she never felt settled enough to commit to one.

This story repeats across the country. When you're juggling multiple part-time roles, moving between jobs frequently, or struggling to cover rent and food costs, pensions feel like a luxury you can't afford right now. But here's the uncomfortable truth: waiting makes things harder later.

The good news? There are steps you can take today, no matter how small your starting point.

**Understand your legal rights.** If you earn over £10,000 annually, your employer must enrol you into a workplace pension scheme and contribute at least 3% of your pay. This applies even if you're part-time. Check whether both your employers are doing this – you might already be saving without realising it.

**Start tiny.** You don't need to save hundreds a month. Even £25 or £50 added to a pension pot each month compounds over decades. Something is infinitely better than nothing.

**Get free help.** MoneyHelper (the UK government's free advice service) has straightforward pension guidance. PensionWise offers free guidance to over-50s. StepChange can help if debt is preventing you from saving.

**Ask your employer.** Many workplaces offer financial wellbeing support or pension advice. It's worth asking what's available.

If you're in a precarious work situation like Sarah, building any pension is an act of self-care for your future self. Start this week – even with a single small contribution.
