If you're wondering whether a YouTube Premium subscription means you can ditch your TV Licence, the answer might surprise you. The short version: Premium doesn't replace a licence if you're watching live TV — but you probably already knew that was too good to be true.

Here's what you actually need to know.

**When you do need a TV Licence**

The rule is straightforward: if you're watching *live* TV programmes on YouTube (or any channel), you need a valid TV Licence. This applies whether you're watching on your telly, computer, phone or tablet. YouTube Premium doesn't get you around this — it only removes ads and adds features like background play.

The same rule applies to other streaming services. You need a licence to watch live programmes on Netflix and Amazon Prime Video, though on-demand content is fine without one. Disney+ content is all on-demand, so you don't need a licence there at all.

**What counts as "live TV"?**

This is the key bit. You need a licence for live-streamed TV broadcasts — the kind of programmes you'd normally see on BBC, ITV, Channel 4 and other channels. User-generated content, clips, and videos don't require one. Neither does live-streamed content that isn't part of a TV broadcast.

**How much does it cost?**

A TV Licence costs £180 per year for a colour TV (as of April 2026). If you only have a black-and-white set — increasingly rare — it's £60.50 annually.

**What you get for your money**

Your licence covers you for watching and recording live programmes across all the major UK channels, plus paid services like Sky, Virgin Media and EE TV, and live content on streaming platforms. It also gives you full access to BBC iPlayer.

**What to do next**

If you watch live TV on any channel or service, check your TV Licence is up to date at tvlicensing.co.uk. If you don't have one and you're watching live content, you could face a fine. And remember: Premium subscriptions are about convenience and features — not legal requirements.
