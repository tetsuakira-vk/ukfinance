Getting on the property ladder has become a mountain to climb for many young British families. But a growing number are turning to rent-to-buy schemes as an alternative route to homeownership — and it's working.

Luke and Hollie Brayne, both 24, recently moved into their three-bedroom dream home in West Sussex with their son. The catch? They won't actually own it for five years. Instead, they're using a rent-to-buy agreement, where they rent the property now with the option to purchase it later.

**What is rent to buy?**

Rent-to-buy schemes allow renters to live in a property while building up a deposit over a fixed period (usually 3–5 years). A portion of the monthly rent goes towards a future purchase, giving families time to improve their credit score, save additional funds, and prepare for a mortgage. It's particularly attractive to those who can't quite afford a traditional deposit right now but want to commit to a specific home.

**Why are families choosing this route?**

The scheme is becoming popular because it tackles two major barriers to homeownership: the deposit gap and mortgage readiness. Traditional mortgages typically require a 5–10% deposit upfront — a sum many first-time buyers simply don't have. Rent-to-buy removes this immediate pressure while letting families build equity gradually.

Property builders and landlords also benefit. They can offload homes that are difficult to sell through conventional channels, turning slow stock into steady rental income with a sale at the end.

**Is it right for you?**

Before committing, check the small print carefully. Understand exactly what portion of rent counts towards your deposit, whether there are early exit fees, and what happens if property prices fall. Make sure you can afford the rent alongside saving for additional costs like legal fees and surveys.

Getting professional advice before signing is essential. Speak to a mortgage broker or housing adviser to understand the full financial picture and ensure rent-to-buy genuinely suits your circumstances.

If you're struggling to save a deposit, explore all options — government schemes, family help, and specialist lenders — before committing to any long-term rental agreement.
