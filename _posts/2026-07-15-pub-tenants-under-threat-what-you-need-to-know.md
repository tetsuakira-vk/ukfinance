If you're a pub tenant or thinking about becoming one, there's important news you should know about. The UK's biggest pub company, Stonegate, is being investigated by the industry watchdog over concerns it has mistreated its tenants — and the investigation reveals a real problem in how some pubs operate.

Stonegate operates around 4,500 pubs across the UK, including well-known venues like the Cannick Taps on Cannon Street and the Cheshire Cheese on Tower Hill. The Pubs Code Adjudicator (PCA) — the official body that protects pub tenants — says it has "reasonable grounds" to suspect the company has broken the rules. This is a serious allegation that could affect hundreds of people who run these pubs as tenants.

**Why does this matter to you?**

Pub tenancy can be a route to self-employment for many people, but it comes with real financial risks. Tenants typically pay rent to the pub company (the landlord) and are often required to buy beer and other products from them at set prices. If the terms aren't fair or the company breaks the rules, tenants can face serious financial hardship.

The Pubs Code exists to protect tenants from unfair treatment. It gives them rights like the ability to challenge excessive rent and to buy drinks from other suppliers. An investigation like this one suggests those protections may not always be working.

**What should you do?**

If you currently run a pub as a tenant, or you're considering it, make sure you understand your rights under the Pubs Code. You can find full information on the PCA's website. If you believe you're being treated unfairly — whether it's excessive rent, unfair tied product prices, or other breaches — you can make a formal complaint to the adjudicator.

Don't accept unfavourable terms without question. Get legal or financial advice before signing any tenancy agreement. Organisations like the British Institute of Innkeeping can also offer guidance.

Keep an eye on how this investigation develops. If Stonegate has broken the rules, it could set an important precedent for how all pub companies must behave towards their tenants.
