If you've been dropped off at a UK airport recently, you may have noticed a new charge appearing on your bill. Airport drop-off fees have jumped by a third on average over the past year, according to new analysis by the RAC – and they're only getting pricier.

Here's what you need to know and how to avoid paying extra when you next fly.

**How much are we talking about?**

The RAC examined drop-off charges at Britain's 20 busiest airports and found that 16 have increased their fees since last summer. The average cost of using a "kiss-and-fly" zone (the quick drop-off area closest to the terminal) has risen by £1.70 to £7. Gatwick tops the list at £10 for just 10 minutes, while London City Airport charges £8 for five minutes – working out at £1.60 per minute. Birmingham offers the best value at £7 for 15 minutes (47p per minute).

These aren't tiny costs. If you regularly drop off or collect family members from airports, they can quickly add up.

**Why are airports doing this?**

Airports say the charges are designed to reduce congestion and air pollution around terminals, and to encourage passengers to use public transport instead. Gatwick, for example, is required to get 54% of passengers using trains and buses as a condition of expanding to a second runway.

However, critics argue the charges are simply another way for airports to make money from a captive audience.

**How to avoid paying**

The good news? You have options. Most airports still offer free drop-off at long-stay car parks with a shuttle bus service to the terminal – though this takes longer. Blue Badge holders are typically exempt from charges. You could also encourage passengers to use public transport, park further away, or use ride-sharing services instead.

Check your specific airport's website before your next trip to understand what's on offer.
