If your landlord is arranging a constant stream of viewings while you're still living in the property, you're not alone in feeling frustrated — and the law is more firmly on your side than you might think.

According to legal advice from Dean Dunham KC, tenants enjoy what's known as the "covenant of quiet enjoyment" and the right to "exclusive possession" of their home throughout their tenancy. This means the flat is legally yours to occupy until the day you move out. Your landlord and their agent cannot simply march prospective tenants through your living room whenever they please.

**What does the law actually say?**

Many tenancy agreements do include a clause allowing viewings during the final weeks before you leave. However, even if yours does, the landlord must still give you reasonable notice — typically at least 24 hours in writing — and arrange visits at reasonable times. A viewings clause is not a licence to disrupt your evenings and weekends continuously.

If your tenancy agreement contains no viewings clause at all, you are within your rights to refuse all viewings until you leave.

**How to take back control**

If a clause does exist and you want to limit the disruption, write to both your landlord and agent. Keep it polite but firm: explain that the frequency is unreasonable and disruptive, then propose sensible alternatives. For example, you might suggest one viewing slot per week at a time that suits you, with 24 hours' notice each time.

A calm, reasonable letter setting clear boundaries is your most effective tool. Document everything in writing so you have a record if things escalate.

**What to do next**

If your landlord continues to ignore your requests, contact Shelter or Citizens Advice for free guidance. You can also escalate a complaint to your local authority's private renting team if necessary.

Your home should remain yours until you leave — don't let constant viewings wear you down.
