If you're claiming Personal Independence Payment (PIP) — or thinking about applying — you should know that a major new government review has found serious problems with how the system works. A report by DWP minister Sir Stephen Timms describes the claims process as "dehumanising", "soul destroying", and "degrading" for disabled people trying to access the support they're entitled to.

This matters because PIP is one of the most important disability benefits in the UK, helping hundreds of thousands of people with extra costs linked to their health condition or disability. For many claimants, this money is essential to their monthly budget.

**What the review found**

The Timms review has highlighted that the current process for claiming and renewing PIP causes unnecessary stress and hardship. Many disabled people report feeling their experiences aren't properly understood, that assessments feel clinical and dismissive, and that the whole experience leaves them feeling worse rather than supported.

**What this means for you**

If you currently claim PIP, the review's findings could eventually lead to changes in how the DWP handles claims and assessments — though this will likely take time. For now, the most important thing is knowing your rights and getting support if your claim is rejected or your benefit is being reassessed.

**Where to get help**

If you're struggling with a PIP claim, don't face it alone. Charities like Citizens Advice, Scope, and StepChange offer free support to help you navigate the system, prepare for assessments, and appeal decisions if needed. Many local councils also run welfare advice services.

If PIP forms part of your income and you're worried about your claim, contact your local Citizens Advice bureau or disability charity now — they can help you understand what you're entitled to and what evidence to gather.

The government has committed to improving the PIP system, but in the meantime, getting expert support early can make a real difference to your chances of success.
