Starting university is exciting, but it also means managing your money independently for the first time. Choosing the right bank account can make student life easier and help you avoid unnecessary charges — so it's worth getting it right from the start.

Most high street banks offer dedicated student accounts, and they're designed with you in mind. The key features to look out for include an interest-free overdraft, which lets you borrow a small amount without paying extra charges. This can be a lifeline when funds run low between loans or part-time wages. However, overdraft limits vary between banks, so compare what's on offer. Some accounts offer £1,000 to £3,000 interest-free borrowing, while others are more generous.

Look for accounts with no monthly fees — you shouldn't have to pay just to have a student account. Many banks will waive charges while you're studying, but this typically ends once you graduate, so check the small print. It's also worth checking whether the bank offers a railcard discount or cashback on everyday spending, as these perks can add up over three years.

Opening a student account is straightforward. You'll usually need proof of your student status (your university acceptance letter or student ID), a form of ID like a passport, and proof of address. You can open most accounts online or in branch — online is usually faster.

Before you commit, compare accounts using tools like MoneySuperMarket or Which?. Check customer reviews too; you want a bank that's reliable and easy to contact if something goes wrong. If you already have a personal account, ask your current bank whether they offer a student version — switching might be simpler than starting elsewhere.

Once you've chosen, set up online banking and mobile alerts so you can track your spending. This will help you stick to your student budget and avoid overdraft fees creeping up.

Start this comparison now, before you arrive at university. It's one less thing to worry about once term begins, and a good account will support your finances throughout student life.
