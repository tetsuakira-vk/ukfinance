First-time buyers in the UK are facing a perfect storm of rising costs, and now major housebuilders are sounding the alarm. Bellway, one of the country's largest new-build developers, has publicly urged the Government to cut stamp duty and introduce a deposit support scheme—a sign that the housing market is in real trouble.

Here's what's happening: buying a home has become significantly more expensive for ordinary people. Stamp duty (the tax you pay when buying a property) can add thousands to the cost of a purchase. At the same time, mortgage rates have climbed following rising oil and gas prices linked to global instability, making monthly payments less affordable. And for builders, the cost of materials, fuel, and labour keeps rising—costs they're passing on to buyers.

Bellway's chief executive has warned of "prolonged softer customer demand" and flagged that the near-term outlook is "uncertain." The company expects profits at the lower end of its forecast, and its order book has shrunk from 5,307 homes to 4,206—a clear sign fewer people are able or willing to buy right now.

What does this mean for you? If you're a first-time buyer, the environment is challenging but not impossible. Stamp duty relief for first-time buyers currently applies to properties up to £425,000, but even with this, costs add up quickly. Higher mortgage rates mean monthly payments are more expensive than they were a year or two ago.

The Government—under Andy Burnham—has ruled out stamp duty changes at the Autumn Budget, so don't expect immediate relief there.

What you can do now: get your finances in order before approaching a lender. A bigger deposit means lower monthly payments. Speak to a mortgage broker (they're free) to understand what you can realistically afford. And if you're saving for a deposit, consider a Lifetime ISA if you're between 18 and 40—the Government adds 25% to your savings, up to £1,000 per year.

For more help with mortgages and first-time buyer schemes, visit MoneyHelper (the Government's free guidance service) or speak to Citizens Advice.
