If you own your home and are fed up with strangers parking outside it, you might be wondering whether you can legally put up a "no parking" sign or traffic cones to keep them away. The good news is that you often can — but there are important rules you need to follow first.

The key question is: who actually owns the land outside your house? This might sound obvious, but it's crucial. Some homeowners own the pavement and verge in front of their property, while in other cases it belongs to the local council, a management company, or all residents jointly. You'll find this information in your home's title deeds or Land Registry records — it's worth checking before you do anything.

If you own the land, you can usually put up a sign saying parking is not permitted or that the area is private. The sign itself doesn't give you legal rights, but it warns motorists that parking without permission could count as trespass. In many cases, a clear, polite sign is enough to stop unwanted parking without any confrontation.

Traffic cones are a bit trickier. You can place them on land you own to discourage parking, but only if they don't block anyone else's right of way or interfere with legal access. Never put cones on land you don't own or on a public highway — this could get you into trouble.

If someone parks anyway and ignores your sign, don't be tempted to take matters into your own hands. Never block a car in, clamp it, or arrange for it to be towed away yourself. This could expose you to serious legal liability and could even lead to confrontation.

The most practical approach is a combination of a clear, polite sign and working together with your neighbours or a residents' management company if you have one. A co-ordinated neighbourhood approach often works better than going it alone.

For detailed advice on your specific situation, check your property deeds or contact your local Land Registry office. If parking disputes escalate, consider seeking legal advice.
