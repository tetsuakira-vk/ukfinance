A 19-year-old from Kent recently lost £650 to an eBay scammer — but his determination to track down the fraudsters offers vital lessons for anyone selling items online.

Blake Walker listed his Apple MacBook on eBay on 29 June and received what looked like a genuine email from eBay confirming the buyer had paid. The catch? The money would only be released once he'd posted the laptop. Blake trusted the message and sent the computer by recorded delivery to an address in Tilbury, Essex. When payment never arrived and eBay confirmed the email was fake, he realised he'd fallen victim to a common online scam.

What makes Blake's story unusual is that he didn't give up. After police told him they couldn't act quickly, he and his mum drove 70 miles to the address on the delivery confirmation. They discovered it was a restaurant being used as a drop-off point for stolen goods — parcels were placed on a fridge and collected by someone else, with evidence suggesting items were later shipped abroad to Nigeria.

**How to protect yourself on eBay:**

Always be suspicious of emails asking you to send goods before payment clears. Legitimate eBay messages come through your account dashboard, not your email inbox. If you receive an email claiming to be from eBay, log into your account directly (don't click email links) to verify any payment claims.

Never post an item until the payment has fully cleared in your account — not just appeared pending. If a deal seems rushed or the buyer pressures you, that's a red flag.

Consider using Royal Mail's Special Delivery Guaranteed by 1pm service, which provides proof of delivery. Keep all documentation.

Blake eventually recovered his laptop, but only through his own efforts. Essex Police and other forces involved didn't prioritise the case. If you're scammed, report it to Action Fraud (the UK's national fraud reporting service) via actionfraud.police.uk, as well as eBay and your bank. Document everything for your records.

Selling online can be safe — just stay alert.
