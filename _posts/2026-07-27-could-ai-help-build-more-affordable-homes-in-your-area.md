Councils across the UK are turning to artificial intelligence and advanced technology to find land where new homes could be built — and it could affect housing availability and prices where you live.

The idea is simple but potentially powerful. Rather than relying on traditional planning methods, councils are using AI to analyse vast amounts of local data. The technology scans information about unused or underutilised land, identifies sites suitable for development, and flags opportunities that might otherwise be missed. For a country facing a serious shortage of affordable homes, this could be a game-changer.

Why does this matter to you? The UK is struggling with a housing crisis. House prices have soared far beyond what many first-time buyers can afford, and rental costs are climbing too. If councils can identify more buildable land quickly and efficiently, it could help increase the supply of new homes — which might eventually ease pressure on prices and rents in your area.

However, there are questions worth asking. How is this data being collected and used? Which councils are already doing this? And what safeguards exist to ensure the technology is used fairly and transparently?

If you're interested in what's happening in your local area, here's what you can do. Contact your local council directly and ask whether they're using AI or tech tools for land identification. You can find contact details on your council's website. Most councils are also required to publish their local plan — a document setting out where they expect homes to be built over the next 15 years. Check this to see what's planned near you.

For those struggling with current housing costs, remember there's support available. If you rent and your income is low, you may qualify for housing benefit or help through Universal Credit. If you're a first-time buyer, look into schemes like the Help to Buy scheme (if you're eligible) or shared ownership programmes that can make getting on the property ladder more achievable.

Keep an eye on your council's plans. Understanding what's coming to your area helps you make better decisions about your home, whether you're buying, renting, or planning for the future.
