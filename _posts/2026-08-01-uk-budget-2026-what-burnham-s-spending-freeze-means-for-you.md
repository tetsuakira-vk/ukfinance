The government has just sent a clear message to its ministers: there's no money to splash about. Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Chancellor John Healey have told cabinet members to stick within existing spending limits while funding new pledges — and this matters to your wallet.

Here's what's happening. Burnham promised voters a series of cost-of-living helps when he took office, including scrapping VAT from domestic electricity bills, cutting business rates for pubs, and capping bus fares across England. But those promises come with a catch: the Treasury has no new money to hand out. Everything new must be funded from existing budgets.

Why does this matter to you? Because it signals that the government is serious about sticking to strict spending rules inherited from the previous Labour administration under Sir Keir Starmer. That means less room for extra support, and more pressure to fund policies through difficult choices — either cutting spending elsewhere or raising taxes.

The Chancellor's first Budget is set for 28 October 2026, and Healey has made it clear that fiscal discipline will be the watchword. Treasury sources have confirmed the government wants to stay "well within the fiscal rules" despite persistent inflation caused by the ongoing war in Iran, which is pushing up borrowing costs.

The challenge is real. Think tanks have warned that Burnham's priorities — including extra defence spending and better social care — may require either spending cuts or tax rises to meet the government's own fiscal rules. The previous chancellor left a £22 billion buffer, but inflation could have eaten into it.

What should you do? Keep an eye on the Budget announcement on 28 October. That's when the government will reveal which of Burnham's pledges are actually funded and how. If you're relying on cost-of-living support like lower energy bills or capped bus fares, watch for confirmed dates and eligibility details. For broader financial planning, remember that any tax rises or spending cuts could affect public services you rely on.

Stay tuned to the official government website and the BBC for full Budget details when they're announced.
