If your neighbour has turned their home into a holiday let and it's become a noise nightmare, you're not alone — and there are real steps you can take to get relief.

A reader recently wrote to us about exactly this problem. Their neighbour's Airbnb, complete with an outdoor hot tub, was hosting large groups and causing disruption at all hours. Guests were parking across pavements, and the noise — especially late into the night during summer — was making sleep impossible. The council had already issued an antisocial behaviour warning, but little else had happened.

Here's what you need to know about your options.

**Report it as a statutory nuisance**

The most powerful tool available to you is getting your council's environmental health department to classify the disruption as a "statutory nuisance". For this to work, the noise must unreasonably and substantially interfere with your use and enjoyment of your home, or risk injuring your health.

If they agree, the council can issue an abatement notice. This legally requires whoever is responsible — including the property owner, even if they don't live there — to stop or restrict the noise. This carries real weight.

**Keep detailed records**

Before taking any action, start documenting everything. Write down dates, times, and what happened. Note how the noise affected you (loss of sleep, stress, etc.). This evidence is crucial when dealing with councils or potentially taking further action.

**Try direct contact first**

Write a formal letter to the property owner explaining the problems and their impact on your household and neighbours. Ask them to implement stricter house rules for guests — quiet hours, hot tub restrictions, and parking guidelines. Sometimes this works without needing council involvement.

**Explore other angles**

You can also push the council for a formal antisocial behaviour case review. The rules vary by council, so check your local authority's website for specific requirements. Note that planning permission rules for Airbnbs differ across councils — yours may have different rules from neighbouring areas.

**Get help from Airbnb**

Airbnb has a Neighbourhood Support scheme where you can report nuisance properties online or request a callback. It's worth trying, though don't expect them to intervene in real time.

If the situation persists, consider consulting a solicitor — many offer free initial advice on neighbour disputes and nuisance claims.
