With summer temperatures climbing and heatwaves becoming more frequent, it's worth thinking ahead about keeping your home cool – and your energy bills in check.

During the last major heatwave, searches for air conditioners jumped by a staggering 1,220%. That surge in demand typically means two things: products sell out fast, and prices creep up. By shopping now, before the next hot spell hits, you can avoid panic-buying at inflated prices and take time to find the best deal for your budget.

**What cooling options should you consider?**

Air conditioning units aren't the only solution. Depending on your home and budget, you might explore portable AC units, window-mounted systems, fans, or even simpler fixes like thermal blinds and decent ventilation. Portable units are often cheaper upfront (usually £200–£800) than fixed installations, though they're less efficient. Window-mounted systems cost more but cool spaces faster and use less energy.

**The energy bill angle**

Air conditioning does increase electricity use, which matters when energy prices are already a concern for many households. If you're considering AC, look for units with high energy efficiency ratings (check the EER or SEER rating). Fans use a fraction of the energy but only work if temperatures aren't extreme.

**Shop smart**

Compare prices across retailers now rather than waiting for July or August when demand peaks. Check what's actually in stock – the Mirror article highlights "restocked" items, suggesting supplies were previously stretched. Read reviews carefully, especially about noise levels and installation requirements.

**What to do next**

If you're renting, check your tenancy agreement or contact your landlord before installing any cooling system – you may need permission. Homeowners should consider whether a fixed installation makes financial sense long-term, or if portable options suit your needs better.

Start browsing now, set a budget, and don't wait for the heatwave warning to hit your phone. Planning ahead means you'll stay comfortable without overpaying.
