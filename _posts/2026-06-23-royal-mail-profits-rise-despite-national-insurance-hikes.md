Royal Mail has reported a revenue increase of 2.6% to £8.4 billion in the year to March 2024, proving that even with rising employment costs hitting businesses across the UK, some large organisations can still grow their top line. But what does this mean for ordinary people relying on postal services?

The postal service's results highlight a challenge facing many UK employers right now: national insurance contributions have gone up significantly, putting pressure on wage bills. For Royal Mail specifically, these employment cost increases haven't stopped the company from pushing ahead, though operating profit remained essentially flat at just £5 million.

This is important context for anyone worried about how the government's employment tax changes are affecting the wider economy. When major employers like Royal Mail can absorb these costs and still grow revenue, it suggests the UK jobs market remains reasonably resilient — at least for now.

However, the flat operating profit tells a different story. While Royal Mail's total income is rising, it's not translating into bottom-line growth. The company faces ongoing challenges including declining letter volumes as more people switch to digital communication. This is a trend that's been accelerating for years and affects everyone who still relies on the post.

If you're a Royal Mail customer, these results suggest the service is managing its finances, though there's limited room for investment in improving delivery reliability — something many readers have complained about.

For employees and job seekers, Royal Mail remains one of the UK's largest employers. The company's ability to weather rising national insurance costs shows that established businesses with scale can adapt, even if margins get tighter.

The broader lesson here is that national insurance increases, while painful for smaller businesses and self-employed people, don't automatically threaten larger employers' viability. But they do limit growth potential and investment.
