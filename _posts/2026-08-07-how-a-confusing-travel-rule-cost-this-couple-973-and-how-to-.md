A UK couple has lost nearly £1,000 on their dream holiday to Turkey after being stopped at the airport gates – and the reason might surprise you.

Peter Wiles and his wife Rogelyn Caresosa had booked flights with Turkish Airlines to Cappadocia, but were denied boarding at Manchester Airport. Turkish Airlines refused to let them fly, leaving them £973 out of pocket and their holiday in ruins.

The couple fell foul of a rule they didn't know existed. While the specific reason for the airline's decision hasn't been fully detailed, travel experts say this kind of situation usually stems from documentation issues – often related to visa requirements, passport validity, or entry requirements that passengers simply aren't aware of beforehand.

This is a cautionary tale about how easy it is to lose money on holiday bookings when you don't do your homework.

**What you need to check before you fly**

Before booking any international trip, spend time on the UK Foreign Office website (gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice). It lists exactly what documents each country requires – visa needs, passport validity periods, and any other entry rules. Turkey, for example, requires British passport holders to have at least six months validity remaining.

Check with your airline too. Contact them directly or visit their website to confirm all documentation needed. Don't assume; ask.

**What to do if this happens to you**

If you're denied boarding, you may have legal rights under UK and EU consumer protection rules. Airlines must explain why they've refused you and may owe you compensation depending on the circumstances. The Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) and Citizen's Advice can help you understand what you're entitled to.

Keep all documentation – your booking confirmation, boarding pass, airline communications, and receipts for any money you've lost. This evidence matters if you need to claim compensation or take a dispute further.

**Moving forward**

Before your next trip, use a travel checklist: passport validity, visa requirements, travel insurance, and airline requirements. Spend 30 minutes now to save yourself £973 later.

For help with travel disputes, contact the Civil Aviation Authority or Citizens Advice Consumer Service.
