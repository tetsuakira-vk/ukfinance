If you heat your home with oil, there's potentially good news: the UK's competition regulator is investigating heating oil suppliers and pushing them to compensate customers who were hit with sky-high bills when prices spiked at the start of the Middle East conflict.

The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) has launched a formal probe into whether suppliers treated customers fairly during the price surge. Some households saw their bills triple compared to what they'd normally pay — a genuinely eye-watering jump that left many people struggling.

**What's happened so far**

When tensions in the Middle East escalated, global oil prices shot up. Heating oil suppliers passed these costs straight on to customers, and some didn't handle it particularly fairly. The CMA spotted the problem and decided to investigate whether suppliers broke competition rules or treated customers unfairly. Now, they're pushing suppliers to compensate those who were overcharged.

**What this means for you**

If you use heating oil to heat your home and you noticed a massive spike in your bills around the time of the Middle East conflict, you may be owed money back. The key thing to know is that you probably don't need to do anything yet — the regulator is working directly with suppliers to arrange compensation. However, it's worth keeping records of your bills from that period, just in case you need to make a claim.

**Next steps**

Keep an eye on any communications from your heating oil supplier. They should be reaching out to affected customers with details of how compensation will work. If you haven't heard anything and think you were unfairly charged, contact your supplier directly and ask about compensation related to the conflict-driven price spike.

You can also check the CMA's website for updates on the investigation, or contact Citizens Advice if you need help understanding your rights.
